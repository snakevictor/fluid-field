# R1: Depth and obstacle map around the player

Spike R1 from `ROADMAP.md`. Status: **recommendation, awaiting a decision.**
Date: 2026-10-13. Phase 1.

**Question:** what is the cheapest accurate map of riverbed, shore and
obstacles around the player? The map feeds the player-centred, scrolling 2D
shallow-water grid.

**Budget:** 0.1 ms amortized, CPU and GPU combined.

**Inputs read:** `reports/skylighting.md`, `reports/unified-water.md` and
`reports/snow-deformation.md`, plus `LEARNINGS.md` and `DECISIONS.md`. Four
research passes then covered:

-   Skylighting's overhead capture
-   engine and literature techniques
-   CPU data available through CommonLib
-   the Snow Deformation fork's height capture

Their working notes were scratch files; everything that matters is condensed
here.

**Conventions:**

-   Paths are relative to the repo root.
-   `CL/` is `extern/CommonLibSSE-NG/include/RE/` (CommonLib v6.7.1, `70c1acd5`).
-   **UNVERIFIED** marks anything inferred rather than read in source, headers
    or a primary document.
-   No figure in this note was measured. Every cost is an estimate until the
    prototypes below run under Tracy.

## 1. What the simulation needs

For each grid texel the map must hold:

| Field          | Meaning                                                         | Why                                                           |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bed height     | Top of the solid ground or riverbed under (or instead of) water | Water depth = surface − bed; wet/dry fronts                   |
| Surface height | Vanilla water plane Z at this texel, or none                    | Where water exists, initial depth, blending with vanilla (R3) |
| Obstacle flag  | Solid static that blocks flow (rock, pier post, wall)           | Reflective boundary for the solver (R2)                       |

Section 6 defines what counts as an obstacle. Actors and loose objects are
out of scope here; they arrive as Phase 2 inputs.

## 2. Recommendation (ranked)

### 1st: CPU data map ("Prototype B")

Build the map from game data instead of rendering. It has four parts.

**Bed from landscape heights.**

-   Copy `TESObjectLAND::loadedData->heights[4][289]` for each attached cell
    (`CL/T/TESObjectLAND.h:62-82`): 4 quadrants of 17×17 vertices, so 33×33 per
    4096-unit cell at 128-unit spacing.
-   Copy each cell once when it attaches, into a Fluid Field-owned cell cache,
    then resample into the world-aligned grid.
-   The vertex and quadrant layout is already confirmed by CS code
    (`src/EngineFixes/LandscapeSeamsFix.cpp:45-48`, `:555-558`).
-   Landscape vertices continue under water, so this is a true riverbed for
    terrain. The Snow Deformation fork relies on the same fact for its bed
    (`reports/snow-deformation.md`).

**Surface from vanilla water objects.**

-   Walk `TESWaterSystem::waterObjects` (`CL/T/TESWaterSystem.h:46`) under its
    `lock` (`:85`).
-   Each `TESWaterObject` carries an `NiPlane plane` and
    `BSTArray<NiPointer<BSMultiBoundAABB>> multiBounds`
    (`CL/T/TESWaterObject.h:27-36`).
-   Rasterize each AABB footprint at its plane height. CS already does a
    point-in-AABB walk over this list and filters sloped water with
    `size.z <= 10` (`src/Features/Skin.cpp:294-329`).
-   Unified Water removes its LOD tiles from this list
    (`src/Features/UnifiedWater.cpp:720-728`), so it holds only near water,
    which is the set we want.
-   Fall back to `TESObjectCELL::GetExteriorWaterHeight()` per cell
    (`CL/T/TESObjectCELL.h:209`). In interiors, read `waterHeight` directly,
    because that function returns −inf there.

**Statics from downward raycasts, only where there is water.**

-   For each newly exposed texel with a water surface, cast one ray from
    `surface + clearance` (e.g. +256) down to `surface − maxDepth` (e.g. −1024).
    Use `TES::Pick(bhkPickData&)` (`CL/T/TES.h:89`) or `hkpWorld::CastRay`
    (`CL/H/hkpWorld.h:96`).
-   Keep the first hit on the static layers `kStatic`, `kAnimStatic`, `kTrees`,
    `kTerrain` and `kGround` (`CL/C/CollisionLayers.h`). **Correction
    (2026-10-14, `reports/r1-route-run.md`):** landscape collision comes back
    on `kGround` (17) with no reference, not on `kTerrain` (13).
-   ~~Starting the ray just above the water skips bridges, docks and canopies by
    construction.~~ **Correction (2026-10-14):** this is wrong as built. Rays
    start 256 units above the water so that rocks rising out of it register,
    and that also catches low bridge and pier decks (`reports/r1-route-run.md`
    section 5). The fix: when the first hit is above the water, a short ray up
    from just under the surface finds any underside below the top. An
    underside means air over the water (deck, arch), so a third ray from just
    above the surface finds the real bed. No underside means a solid rising
    out of the water (rock, pier), which stays an obstacle. The hit height
    gives the bed directly:
    -   above the surface: dry obstacle or shore
    -   below: water depth
-   The result is max(landscape bed, ray hit).

**Incremental, cached, data-driven.**

-   The grid is world-aligned with toroidal addressing. Only the strip exposed
    by scrolling is computed, and results are cached per world texel, since
    statics do not move.
-   Invalidate on cell attach/detach and worldspace change. Unified Water
    already detects the latter (`src/Features/UnifiedWater.cpp:537-546`).
-   Upload dirty rows and columns only.
-   Poll `TES::gridCells` on a grid change instead of hooking. That matches the
    roadmap rule "inputs are data, not hooks". The 1.7.99+ terrain-cell lock
    (`REL::ID(564236)`) must wrap the lookup, as Unified Water does
    (`src/Features/UnifiedWater.cpp:593-603`).

**Why first:**

-   It is view-independent: obstacles behind the camera and upstream are
    present.
-   It needs no render-pass hooks and touches none of the contested call sites
    (section 3).
-   It handles bridges with one or two extra short rays where something is
    above the water (corrected 2026-10-14; first written as "for free").
-   It works in interiors, using rays plus cell water.
-   It is accurate where it matters physically, since water collides with what
    the player collides with.

**Estimated cost (UNVERIFIED):**

-   Landscape copy: about 5–20 µs per cell, once per attach.
-   Water-object walk: under 10 µs per rebuild.
-   Rays: at about 2–5 µs each, a 128² grid with a 64-unit ray lattice needs
    roughly 16 rays per frame at sprint, which fits 0.1 ms.
-   A 256² grid at full 32-unit ray density does not fit (about 0.13–0.64 ms
    per frame).
-   A full refill after a teleport is about 33–80 ms for 128², so it must be
    time-sliced or hidden behind the loading screen.

**Main risks:**

-   Collision shapes are coarser than visuals, so a rock may look different
    from how water flows around it.
-   Ray cost and thread safety in our hook context are unmeasured.
-   Sloped placed water (rapids, waterfalls) is excluded by the `size.z` filter.

### 2nd: GPU top-down depth capture ("Prototype A")

Render our own straight-down orthographic depth pass through the engine's
precipitation-occlusion path, the way Skylighting does.

**How the path works:**

-   **Entry:** Skylighting hooks the call at
    `RelocationID(35560, 36559) + 0x3A1` (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:314`).
-   **What it renders:**
    -   It runs `Precipitation::SetupMask`/`RenderMask` with its own camera, depth
        target and culling (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:535-560`, `:571-652`).
    -   It is depth-only: `BSUtilityShader` `RenderDepth` in render mode
        `kPrecipitationOcclusionMap = 0x1C` (`CL/B/BSShaderAccumulator.h:31`,
        `src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:468-493`).
-   **Re-entrant:** Skylighting already runs it twice a frame with save and
    restore around it, which shows it can be run again.

**Fluid Field's capture:**

-   **Own target and camera:** its own DSV, direction `(0,0,-1)`, and a box the
    size of the grid.
-   **Own filter mode:**
    -   Fluid Field gets its own mode in the `GetRenderPasses_Occlusion` (vfunc
        0x2D) override.
    -   It must let terrain through. Skylighting admits terrain only when its own
        flag is set (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:427-429`).
    -   It must drop canopy, foliage and editor markers.
-   **Clipped frustum:** a near plane clamped just above the local water level,
    set through the existing frustum hook at `25643 + 0x5D9`
    (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:316`). This is the "thin height band" idea
    from Batman: Arkham Origins (GDC 2014, see sources).
-   **Strip-only redraw:** each frame, only the newly exposed strip is drawn
    (GPU Gems 2 ch. 2 toroidal update). The whole box is refilled over several
    frames after a teleport.

**Why second:**

-   It captures the true visual geometry, including non-colliding meshes.
-   It scales to grids of 256² and above better than rays.
-   It is the dominant technique in shipped engines:
    -   UE WaterZone's water info texture
    -   Crest's seabed depth cache
    -   UE distance-field and virtual-shadow-map incremental updates

**Main risks:**

-   **Crowded call chain:** the hook site at `+0x3A1` is already chained
    Upscaling → Skylighting. Upscaling's `dynamicResolutionLock` must be set by
    hand when calling outside that chain.
-   **Crash history:**
    -   This pass is where the alpha-group pool overflow crash happened (#2675).
    -   Skylighting grows the pool from 512 to 1024
        (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:675-728`). Without Skylighting, Fluid Field
        loses that guard.
-   **Interiors:** the path is skipped in interiors
    (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:532`), so they would need Prototype B anyway.
-   **CPU cost:** the CPU cost of submitting draws dominates, not the GPU.
-   **Unknown target properties:** the depth format, size and near/far planes
    are not in source. Reading them needs a RenderDoc capture.
-   **Hidden riverbed:** without the clipped near plane, the depth is the top
    surface: bridges, canopy and roof markers would hide the riverbed.

### Combining them

The two are not exclusive. The recommended plan is B as the Phase 1 map. A
stays a drop-in replacement for the static and obstacle layer if B's accuracy
or cost fails, or for grids larger than 128². Both write the same three-field
map (section 1), so the solver never knows which one produced it.

## 3. Question 1: Skylighting's overhead view

-   **How it works:**
    -   Skylighting does **not** call the vanilla function while the shader cache
        is on. It reimplements it (`src/Features/Skylighting.cpp:523-560`).
    -   It renders one quadrant of a 10000-unit box per frame, cycling over 4
        frames (`:497-513`, `:502`, `:569`). The quadrant size is inferred from the
        commit history (**UNVERIFIED**).
    -   Its view direction is randomly tilted up to Max Zenith (default 90°;
        `:607-617`).
    -   The output is a sky-occlusion depth, overwritten every frame.
-   **Geometry included:**
    -   lighting-shader meshes with bound radius > 32 (`:425-429`)
    -   terrain, in Skylighting's mode only
    -   trees and alpha-tested foliage (`:468-493`)
    -   editor markers such as roof markers, since #2807 (`git show ca63a41d5`)
-   **Geometry excluded:**
    -   small rocks (radius ≤ 32)
    -   actors
    -   Havok clutter
    -   grass and effects (their shader properties emit no occlusion passes)
-   **Cost:** no figures in code, commits or PRs. Baselines to read in Tracy:
    -   the zones "Skylighting Mask" (`:563`) and "Skylighting - Render Height Map"
        (`:628`)
    -   `ZoneScopedS` on `RenderOcclusion` (`:517`)
-   **Reuse or share:**
    -   (a) Reading its texture: rejected (section 4).
    -   (b) Running our own capture through the same engine functions: feasible.
        That is Prototype A.
    -   (c) A shared top-down capture service for Skylighting, Fluid Field and
        later snow: the right long-term shape, but it needs maintainer buy-in to
        refactor Skylighting and saves no GPU work. Not needed for Phase 1.

## 6. Question 4: what counts as an obstacle

**Static obstacles in this map:**

-   collision layers `kStatic`, `kAnimStatic`, `kTrees`, `kTerrain` and
    `kGround` for B
-   the matching lighting-shader statics in A's filter: rocks, cliffs, piers,
    docks, walls, tree trunks at the water line, and riverbed meshes

**Not in this map:**

-   actors and horses
-   clutter, props and small and large debris (`kClutter`, `kProps`,
    `kDebrisSmall`, `kDebrisLarge`)
-   projectiles
-   water itself

These come from Phase 2 inputs through the emitter API, which already have
positions and Havok sphere bounds (`src/Utils/ActorUtils.cpp`, see
`LEARNINGS.md`).

**Changing statics:** doors, gates and destructibles are `kAnimStatic` and can
change. They are treated as static and refreshed only when their texels are
invalidated (**UNVERIFIED** how often that is wrong; ignored for R1).

**Bridges and overhangs:**

-   Geometry whose underside is above the water surface is not an obstacle; water
    passes under it.
-   B handles this with the upward probe described in section 2 (corrected
    2026-10-14: starting rays above the water alone does not).
-   A handles it with the near-plane band. The fork's "underside more than 40
    units above terrain" rule is the fallback idea if the band is not enough.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Landscape heights:**
    - Are `loadedData->heights` absolute world Z? (**UNVERIFIED**; check
      against `TES::GetLandHeight`, `CL/T/TES.h:86`.)
    - **Answered 2026-10-14** (`reports/r1-stage-a-checks.md`): no. They are
      relative; adding `heightExtents.x - min(heights)` matches
      `TES::GetLandHeight` exactly at every vertex. Cells are dropped when
      they leave the loaded grid; whether the engine frees `loadedData` then
      is still unchecked.
    - Is `loadedData` freed when a cell detaches?
2. **Ray cost and context:**
    - What does one downward ray cost in Riverwood and in Solitude?
    - Is our hook context safe for Havok queries (main thread, not mid-step,
      `bhkWorld::worldLock`, `CL/B/bhkWorld.h:74`)?
    - Is an all-hits collector usable from CommonLib, or must we filter with a
      layer such as `kLOS`? Dump `bhkCollisionFilter::layerBitfields[41]`.
3. **Riverbed meshes:** how much riverbed on the test routes is placed static
   meshes rather than landscape? This decides whether B's rays are needed
   beyond the shoreline.
    - **Answered 2026-10-14** (`reports/r1-stage-b-play-session.md`
      section 4a): landscape is enough for deep water; placed meshes
      (boulders, banks, the bridge) are wrong near the surface, so stage d's
      raycasts are needed before surface disturbance.
4. **Placed water:**
    - Do all placed water objects register `multiBounds`?
    - How should sloped water (rapids, waterfalls) be represented? Overlaps R3.
5. **Several water levels in view:** which surface wins in overlapping AABBs,
   and how does A's near-plane band handle it? Overlaps R3.
6. **Precipitation-occlusion depth target (A):**
    - What are its format, size and near/far planes? Needs RenderDoc.
    - Does water draw into that pass?
7. **Draw cost (A):** what is the CPU cost of submitting draws for a 4096-unit
   strip capture in a city?
8. **Collision against visuals (B):** how visible is the mismatch at a 32-unit
   texel? Decide in game.

## 8. Prototype plan

Prototypes start only after approval. They are throwaway: one branch each
(`ff/r1-proto-b`, `ff/r1-proto-a`), behind a debug-only setting, never merged
as is. The winning approach is rebuilt cleanly in Phase 1.

### Shared setup (both prototypes)

-   The same output: a world-aligned, toroidal 128² map at 32-unit texels
    (4096 units, about one cell) holding bed height, surface height and an
    obstacle flag.
-   A debug overlay drawing the three layers over the scene. The roadmap
    already requires this in Phase 1.
-   Tracy CPU and GPU zones around every step.
-   **Test routes** (from `ROADMAP.md`):
    -   Riverwood river and bridge
    -   Solitude dock
    -   a fast travel
    -   a horse gallop across a cell border
    -   one interior with water (to be picked)
-   **Accuracy reference:** an offline high-density run of each prototype (rays
    every 8 units for B; full-box capture for A), compared with each other and
    with RenderDoc captures.

### Prototype B steps

1. Cell cache:
    - copy landscape heights on grid change, under the terrain-cell lock
    - verify absolute Z against `TES::GetLandHeight` at random points
      (answers open question 1)
2. Water-surface pass: walk `waterObjects` under `lock`, rasterize the AABB
   footprints, cell-water fallback.
3. Downward rays for exposed water texels on a 64-unit lattice:
    - layer filter as in section 6
    - time-sliced with a per-frame ray cap
4. Toroidal upload of dirty rows and columns only.
5. Measurements:
    - ray cost
    - per-frame total at walk, sprint and horse speed
    - teleport refill time
    - wet/dry agreement and bed-height error against the reference

### Prototype A steps

1. A RenderDoc capture of the precipitation-occlusion pass to read the depth
   target's format, size and near/far, and whether water draws. This answers
   open question 6 before any code.
2. Our own depth target and a straight-down camera through
   `SetupMask`/`RenderMask`, with save and restore around them as Skylighting
   does.
3. Our own filter mode in the 0x2D override: terrain in, canopy, foliage and
   markers out.
4. Near-plane clamp at the local water level plus clearance, set through the
   frustum hook.
5. Strip-only redraw, then the same measurements as B, plus draw counts.
   Also run with Skylighting disabled and with Upscaling on.

### Success criteria and decision rule

**Pass if:**

-   amortized cost ≤ 0.1 ms (CPU + GPU) at sprint
-   teleport refill ≤ 1 s, spread so no frame exceeds 2 ms
-   wet/dry agreement ≥ 95% against the reference
-   bed-height error small enough that no visible depth artefact shows on the
    test routes
-   no crash across loading screens or cell transitions
-   bridges never block flow

**Decision:**

-   **If B passes,** adopt B for Phase 1 and keep A as a documented upgrade path
    for larger grids.
-   **If B fails accuracy** (placed riverbed meshes or collision mismatch) **or
    cost,** and A passes, adopt A for the static layer and keep B's landscape and
    water-surface parts. They are needed in every case, including interiors.

The decision goes into `DECISIONS.md` once a human makes the call.
